Hi, "author" of spore here. 

Yes spore is coded by an ai but there is more to it than that, let me give you some context.


A long interest of mine is mesh networks, i have created open infrastructure for heating with builtin mesh networks delivering democratic heating to hundreds of people and have told that story on the stage of ccc.


Why spore: because all mesh protocols i have evaluated through the years are hard to explain to laymans terms. And i live in a place with a lot of not so techy activists that wanna know how shit works. So i thought if the core idea is a signed envelope with the message being readable by the intended receiver only. Then it would be easy to explain. 

Then came ideas of filesharing: how about using fountain codes as a kind of torrent replacement.

Then came ideas about portability: what if the same rust core is used everywhere from esp32 to desktop or web?

Then came the idea of a layered router: the router is responsible for choosing which transports to transmit info to and how to store and forward etc but the transports themselves are responsible for their inner mechanics, a transport is seen as a link by the core router. 

Then came ideas about having a spec for the kernel that could be printed with a decent level of detail on a single double sided piece of paper. 

Then came ideas of reproducability in an offline world. 

So from my point of view it has been a very organic project albeit using claude and other ais to do the coding but with me constantly reading through the codebase and doing the development in public for full disclosure. 

It really is pretty heartfelt from me. Its ok for you to hate vibe coded stuff but now you got the handwritten context from my android phone.  (So sorry about typos)


I am continuing to develop spore as i think its important with something that is compact and completely decoupled from networks.
